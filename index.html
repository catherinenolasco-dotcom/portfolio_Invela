Leadership Philosophy

How I lead, listen, and build — inspired by Hal Halladay's Black Rock framework

🖤 What Are You Not Seeing?

Hal Halladay's TED talk on the Black Rock asks a simple but profound question: what are you not seeing? His insight is that we all look at the same rock — the same team, the same situation, the same data — and see completely different things based on our assumptions, filters, and blind spots.

His challenge to leaders: be curious, not judgmental. Ask what you're missing before you decide. The leaders who get it right aren't the ones with the most answers — they're the ones who ask the best questions.

"I realized I'd been practicing this framework in HR for years without having language for it. When I walk into a new company, I don't assume I know where the gaps are — I ask questions first. That curiosity is what helped me catch compliance violations that had been sitting in plain sight for years."

— Catherine Nolasco
🔍

Curiosity Over Judgment

Before I assess a team, a system, or a situation — I ask questions. What's the history here? What have people tried before? What are they afraid to say out loud? The answers always change what I build.

👁️

See What Others Miss

My compliance catches at Prezent — I-9 deficiencies, Section 135B India voting law violations — weren't lucky finds. They came from proactive auditing and refusing to assume that "no one's complained" means "everything's fine."

🤝

Empathy as Strategy

Understanding how someone sees the rock — their perspective, their fears, their context — is how I build trust fast. At Prezent, my India team had never had a leader who asked how they were doing. Asking changed everything.

🏗️

Build for What's Not There Yet

Most HR leaders see what exists and maintain it. I try to see what's missing — the compliance gap no one mapped, the onboarding friction no one measured, the culture signal no one named. Then I build for it.

🎯 How This Shows Up In My Work

Compliance

At Prezent I asked "what are we not seeing?" in our I-9 records — and found 38 violations that had been invisible because no one had looked. Curiosity-first compliance catches things checklists miss.

Team Leadership

With my Prezent India team, I didn't assume I knew what they needed. I asked. The answer was simple: someone who actually listened. That one shift turned a disengaged team into one that thrived.

HRIS & Systems

When I audit a Rippling setup, I don't start with what's configured — I start with what's missing. Invela's Rippling was "implemented" but nobody asked what wasn't working. That's the rock no one looked at.

M&A Integration

Post-acquisition, everyone focuses on what was handed over. I ask what was left behind — the undocumented policies, the informal norms, the compliance gaps that lived in someone's head and walked out the door.
